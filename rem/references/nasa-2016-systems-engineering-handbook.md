# NASA Systems Engineering Handbook Revision 2

## Key takeaway

Process guidance supplies engineering questions and distinctions; it does not select REM’s metamodel.

## Summary

This is one handbook evidence family, even though several NASA HTML chapters were consulted. Its requirements, integration, assessment, configuration-management and decision-analysis guidance informs this methodology. NASA-specific review structures are not adopted automatically.

## Current inspection and use limit

The six mapped chapters retain the prior inspection dates and extents recorded below. Consolidation inspection on 2026-10-07 additionally checked selected official HTML text in §4.3, §6.5 and §6.8. This is one institutional handbook family, not a full handbook audit or several independent confirmations.

## Located contributions

### Requirements definition

**Source mapping:** S01. [NASA — Technical Requirements Definition](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/4-2-technical-requirements-definition/).

**Locator:** Handbook §4.2, especially recursive definition, requirements rationale and requirements validation.

Supports distinct levels of need and obligation, rationale, and early checking. Does not mandate IR/SR/AR names, an exact depth, or REM cardinalities.

**Retained inspection:** Primary source inspected on 2026-10-06. This note identifies limited supporting reasoning, not approval of REM or its implementation. REM links to the publication without redistributing its text or figures.

### Requirements management

**Source mapping:** S03. [NASA — Requirements Management](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-2-requirements-management/).

**Locator:** Handbook §6.2.1.2.2–6.2.1.2.4.

Supports hierarchical organization, bidirectional traceability and controlled requirement changes. NASA discusses cases beyond REM's selected tree; that discussion does not change REM's exact single-parent IR/SR/AR rules.

**Retained inspection:** Primary source inspected on 2026-10-06. This note identifies limited supporting reasoning, not approval of REM or its implementation. REM links to the publication without redistributing its text or figures.

### Product integration

**Source mapping:** S16. [NASA — Product Integration](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/5-2-product-integration/).

**Locator:** Systems Engineering Handbook, Revision 2, §5.2 introduction and process description.

Integration examines subsystem/environment interactions and adverse emergent behavior. REM uses that concern to motivate composition reasoning; it does not infer that all component tests must be repeated at system level or that compositional proof is insufficient.

**Retained inspection:** Primary source inspected on 2026-10-07 within the scope stated above. This note supports explanatory reasoning, not approval or empirical validation of REM. Examples in the principles are authored illustrations.

### Interface management

**Source mapping:** S17. [NASA — Interface Management](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-3-interface-management/).

**Locator:** Systems Engineering Handbook, Revision 2, §6.3.1.2.5 Capture Work Products and §6.3.1.3 Outputs.

Interface management retains rationale, assumptions, anomaly corrections and interface agreements. REM-authored acknowledgement and retry examples illustrate why compatible semantics matter; the source does not establish REM provider cardinality or a universal retry mechanism.

**Retained inspection:** Primary source inspected on 2026-10-07 within the scope stated above. This note supports explanatory reasoning, not approval or empirical validation of REM. Examples in the principles are authored illustrations.

### Product verification

**Source mapping:** S04. [NASA — Product Verification](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/5-3-product-verification/).

**Locator:** Handbook §5.3, methods and verification reports.

Supports planned assessments, configuration-specific observations and conclusions. A test definition or link does not establish a passing outcome.

**Retained inspection:** Primary source inspected on 2026-10-06. This note identifies limited supporting reasoning, not approval of REM or its implementation. REM links to the publication without redistributing its text or figures.

### Product validation

**Source mapping:** S05. [NASA — Product Validation](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/5-4-product-validation/).

**Locator:** Handbook §5.4, distinction from verification and intended-use environments.

Supports evaluating stakeholder expectations and use context. REM selects its own procedure and representation; this is not a NASA certification process.

**Retained inspection:** Primary source inspected on 2026-10-06. This note identifies limited supporting reasoning, not approval of REM or its implementation. REM links to the publication without redistributing its text or figures.

### Logical decomposition

[Handbook §4.3](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/4-3-logical-decomposition/), introduction and §4.3.1.3, connects logical relationships with derived requirements. REM uses this to motivate explaining contributions from lower obligations; the source does not select REM’s requirement tree. Selected text checked on 2026-10-07.

### Configuration management

[Handbook §6.5](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-5-configuration-management/), introduction and §6.5.1.2.2–3, supports identifiable configurations and controlled changes. Selected text checked on 2026-10-07. REM’s rule that a Git commit alone cannot establish engineering approval remains its own safeguard.

### Decision analysis

[Handbook §6.8](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-8-decision-analysis/), §6.8.1 and §6.8.1.2.1, identifies alternatives, criteria, uncertainty and proportionate analysis. Selected text checked on 2026-10-07. REM applies these concerns in its architecture decision Practice; the source does not prescribe a universal worksheet or questionnaire size.

## Limits and non claims
This record does not establish that REM is NASA-compliant, that NASA endorses REM, or that its practices improve a particular project. Regulatory obligations, actual approval authority, specialized safety analyses and process tailoring need separate project evidence. The consulted pages are not a substitute for an organization’s approved obligations.
