# Gotel and Finkelstein An Analysis of the Requirements Traceability Problem

## Key takeaway

Tracing an approved requirement forward is different from recovering where it came from and how it was formed.

## Summary

This research distinguishes pre-specification and post-specification traceability. It helps REM keep source statements, contributors and reconciliation reasons visible rather than beginning provenance at the final requirement document.

## Located contribution

1994 International Conference on Requirements Engineering, pp. 94–101; §5.2 and Figure 1 in the institutional PDF.

Distinguishes pre-specification traceability of requirement formation from post-specification traceability of subsequent development. REM applies the distinction to origin and realization; its edge types and retention choices are local decisions.

The abstract and §2 describe a mixed investigation involving over 100 practitioners. Text in §§5.2–5.4 distinguishes formation from subsequent development and identifies limitations in origin-side support. The incoming reference also points to §§6–8 for barriers and conclusions; those additional sections are reading leads, not additional adopted findings.

## Independence and limits
This practitioner investigation is distinct from NASA’s later institutional guidance. It is historically situated research, not an estimate of the prevalence of traceability failures in present-day projects. Its sample and methods do not establish an optimal link density or tool.

## What it does not establish
It does not validate REM’s precise vocabulary, require every request to become a requirement, or prove that a dense graph is useful. It supplies evidence for a neglected question, not a mandatory storage scheme.
