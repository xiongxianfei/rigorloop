# Improve REM through actual use

## Key takeaway

Use a real difficulty to revise the smallest relevant explanation or operation, rather than changing the ontology to fit one anecdote.

## Summary

REM can apply its own knowledge distinctions to maintenance. This Practice starts with an observed problem in engineering work, tests competing explanations, checks source support and revises the relevant Concept, Principle, Model, Method or Practice. It keeps project-specific preferences and normative implementation choices separate from reusable knowledge.

## Inputs and local understanding
Use an actual observation, decision record or documented review difficulty. Separate what happened from the explanation first proposed. A Principle is a reusable explanatory relationship; a Model represents interacting meanings; a Method performs one operation; a Practice coordinates a real goal. A rule such as “every SR has one parent” is not an explanatory Principle.

No past project outcomes are prefilled. An editorial inspection of this publication can reveal a documentation defect, but does not demonstrate real engineering effectiveness.

## Stage map
1. Capture the difficulty and context.
2. Diagnose which knowledge role is involved.
3. Inspect support and alternatives.
4. Revise and test local completeness.
5. Publish the qualified change and seek application evidence.

## Stage 1 — Capture without rewriting history
### Stage summary
**Goal:** preserve the problem actually encountered. **Why:** hindsight can make the cause seem obvious. **Do:** record context, available information, action and outcome. **Observe:** interpretation presented as fact. **Success:** a usable evidence boundary. **Next:** compare explanations.

Describe the intended outcome and actual difficulty. Was the missing information a stakeholder answer, source reference, procedure step, local summary or formal implementation decision? Preserve the actual wording and relevant version. Do not turn a frustrating experience into a universal claim about engineering.

**Fallback:** if only a concern is available, label it a hypothesis for review, not an observed failure.

## Stage 2 — Locate the knowledge problem
### Stage summary
**Goal:** revise the correct layer. **Why:** not every defect requires a new Principle. **Do:** test Concept, Principle, Model, Method and Practice explanations. **Observe:** design rules disguised as knowledge. **Success:** a bounded revision target. **Next:** sources and alternatives.

A missing distinction may need a Concept. A wrong explanatory relationship may need a Principle revision. An inaccurate relationship structure may need a Model. An operation lacking inputs or checks may need a Method. A workflow that gives links instead of executable stages may need a Practice rewrite. Current cardinalities, lifecycle and other rules already belong to their governing Models, Methods and project contracts. Apply those rules when diagnosing a gap. A proposed new rule or changed rule requires an explicit, reviewed revision at its governing owner; calling it a deferred specification question does not suspend a current requirement.

**Fallback:** keep several candidate explanations when evidence cannot identify one. Do not force a new folder for every missing detail.

## Stage 3 — Inspect source support
### Stage summary
**Goal:** separate evidence from preference. **Why:** repetition or authority can conceal unsupported inference. **Do:** extract claim-relevant sources and compare them. **Observe:** shared upstream evidence and scope differences. **Success:** an explicit synthesis or unresolved limit. **Next:** revise at the supported level.

Write the exact candidate claim. Search the material needed to establish or challenge it, not a broad bibliography. Record source identity, inspected passage, contribution and limits. Group repeated publications of one argument or chapters of one handbook. Compare definitions, theoretical explanations, empirical observations and practical guidance without treating them as interchangeable.

For a Principle such as “a need can outlive a realization,” explain the relationship and conditions. REM’s selected IR/SR/AR cardinalities are binding within current REM even though other methods could make different choices; they are not conclusions uniquely entailed by the external explanation.

**Fallback:** if the evidence does not support the candidate explanation, keep it provisional or restrict its scope. Do not rescue the claim by citing more pages that repeat the same source.

## Stage 4 — Revise and check self-containment
### Stage summary
**Goal:** improve usable knowledge without uncontrolled duplication. **Why:** readers need local reasoning, while copied explanations can diverge. **Do:** update the canonical object and affected summaries. **Observe:** missing conditions and link-only procedures. **Success:** locally complete, traceable content. **Next:** publish with limits.

Write a Key takeaway, Summary and sufficient role-specific content. A Principle states the relationship, basis and limits. A Model contains the representation. A Method contains inputs, why, steps and checks. A Practice provides stage summaries and executable details. Hide links and attempt the intended reading/use task; restore links and inspect whether the compressed explanation remains faithful.

Update references and any downstream meaning affected. A changed file does not imply every linked object must change, but it does create a review question. Do not copy entire canonical files merely to make summaries longer.

**Fallback:** if an inline summary changes the original claim or loses a safeguard, revise the summary before publication; a working link does not repair the missing meaning.

## Stage 5 — Publish and learn from application
### Stage summary
**Goal:** make the change available without overclaiming it. **Why:** publication checks do not establish field effectiveness. **Do:** record what changed, its basis and remaining tests. **Observe:** structural validation described as successful engineering use. **Success:** an honest release and next real application. **Next:** apply to a bounded change and record results.

Publish only through the actual project authority and delivery process; this Practice itself grants no external publication permission. Distinguish wording corrections, conceptual changes and implementation proposals. Check links, identifiers, references and publication consistency. Describe checks actually performed. Preserve the fact that an untested Method is untested. Try it on an appropriately bounded engineering task and compare its effects before generalizing further.

## Current learning summary
Record what difficulty is established; what explanation is provisional; which objects changed; which sources support the change; which application remains needed; and the next reviewer or task. The template is not a mandatory project schema.

## Basis and deeper knowledge

This self-contained maintenance procedure is REM-authored synthesis using the [declared KPS basis](../metadata.md). That relationship does not establish KPS conformance, publication approval or completed source inspection. [Gotel/Finkelstein](../references/gotel-finkelstein-1994-requirements-traceability-problem.md#located-contribution) informs origin traceability, [Zave/Jackson](../references/zave-jackson-1997-four-dark-corners.md#located-claims-and-inspected-extent) informs the role of assumptions, and NASA [verification](../references/nasa-2016-systems-engineering-handbook.md#product-verification) and [validation](../references/nasa-2016-systems-engineering-handbook.md#product-validation) inform evidence distinctions. No source validates the entire maintenance workflow.
