# Experiment and confidence method

Load this method only when a benchmark, experiment, or non-trivial confidence assessment can materially answer a bounded question.

Define the decision threshold, variables, environment, representative inputs, controls, measurement method, and stopping condition before running an experiment. Prefer the smallest reproducible test that distinguishes the decision-relevant outcomes. Do not generalize beyond the tested environment or conceal warm-up, cache, sampling, failure, or variance effects.

Assess confidence from evidence authority, directness, freshness, consistency, coverage of the relevant environment, and sensitivity to unresolved assumptions. Use plain calibrated labels such as high, medium, or low only when their basis is stated; avoid invented numeric precision.

Record negative or inconclusive results. Stop when another run is unlikely to cross the decision threshold, when environmental control is insufficient, or when the experiment would expand scope or require unavailable authority.
